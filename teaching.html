Teaching

Machine Learning for the Humanities

MA course, Università di Roma Tor Vergata, November 2026 · in Italian · nine 3-hour lessons.

From a first Python program to language models, every lesson pairs a guiding question with a hands-on lab, and comes back to the same thread: happiness in Italian literature (felicità, gioia, allegria, contentezza, letizia, beatitudine) and what a machine can and cannot grasp of it. The course deliberately keeps its failures in view: a perfect author classifier that turns out to recognise books rather than authors, a 98%-accurate detector that finds nothing, a data leak kept as a case study.

Syllabus

  • Python from scratch: linear and logistic regression
    Does happiness rise as Dante climbs towards Paradise?
  • Data and words: decision trees
    Turning texts into numbers; Pinocchio's happiest chapter is a trap.
  • Learning from examples: SVM
    Can a machine discover the rules on its own?
  • Measuring results: baselines, precision, recall
    Is 95% accuracy really good?
  • AI as an assistant: k-means
    Prompting, testing and fixing code written by a chatbot.
  • Meaning and similarity: hierarchical clustering
    Do machines really understand?
  • Creativity, irony and bias: distributional semantics
    What models do not understand.
  • Our experiment: language models
    Answering a real question with data, in small groups.
  • Show and discuss
    What we can build, and what we can criticise.
